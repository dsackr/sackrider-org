Baby's bounce, Adults break, and Seniors shatter...

This weekend I was watching the kids while my wife was out. Suddenly I hear my 8 yr old son Zander yelling "Dad! Dad! Aiden is hurt!"

Aiden is crying as he comes out of Zander's room. I scoop him up and ask Zander, "what happened"

Zander walks me back to his room, points to the third step up his bunk bed's 5 step ladder and says "Aiden fell from here!"

Aiden, still sobbing looks up and quickly yells "NO! NO! NO!"

"That's not where you feel from Aiden?" I asked

"No, I fell from there!" Aiden replies as he points to the top step.

Ok - lets think about this for a second. Aiden is young and when we are young we bounce. Adults break but babies bounce. Aiden is fine - no broken bones, not even a knot on his head but he is crying for 20 mins and wants full credit for his fall - the top step, not the lower one.

Now I'm in my thirties and last month I fell of a dirt bike and broke two ribs. I tore my jeans, damaged the bike so I couldn't ride it, and had to walk 7 miles back to the parking lot. I told me wife what happened, but that I was ok. Like my son, I wanted someone to know but was a bit embarrassed by the fall and certainly not going to cry.

Now consider my wife's grandmother. She is in her nineties; she fell out of her chair at home and broke her hip. Using her cane to pull the phone off the wall, she calls her daughter (my wife's aunt) and has the following conversation:

"Hey Mom, what's up?"

"Are you busy?" with a calm non-alarming voice.

"Not terribly, is everything ok?"

"Well... if you have some time, do you think you could come over? I need a bit of help if its not too much trouble"

If you asked my wife's grandmother why is wasn't more forth coming, she would reply with "I didn't want to be a bother"

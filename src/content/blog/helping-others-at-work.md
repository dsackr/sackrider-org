"Failure to plan on your part, does not constitute an emergency on my part" - WRONG ANSWER

We've all heard it from teachers, coaches, and co-workers. But this comment, as clever as it sounds, doesn't account for common success goals; especially on large projects.

An officer in the Marine Corp once told me "sometimes you have to let them step on their own poncho." But he added you do that in boot camp, in training, but not in battle. Being allowed to suffer the consequences of our mistakes is an important part of life, but lets also remember that sometimes, the middle of a crisis isn't a teaching moment.

Not every situation can be fixed by just adding urgency, but if a problem hits your desk and you can fix it, you should; regardless of who gets credit, who you 'bailed out'.

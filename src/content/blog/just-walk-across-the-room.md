Sunday, my pastor delivered a sermon titled "The Second Greatest Story Ever Told...". I'll give away the punchline in telling you that story is your personal salvation story, second only to the universal salvation story of Christ and Christ crucified. The point of his message was that we must fulfill the Great Commission by sharing our personal story with those around us.

While listening to the message, I thought to myself, "everyone I know is already a Christian. My co-workers, the dads at my son's cub scouts, the moms at my daughters dance class, my neighbors - all Christians of some <em>flavor (baptist, pentecostal, catholic, etc</em>)"... Then I heard the preacher mention a book titled "Just Walk Across the Room: Simple Steps for Pointing People to Faith" by Bill Hybels.

Understand the Great Commission applies to me and wanting to implement this message in my life, I quickly grab my smart phone, open the Audible app, click the shopping cart icon in the top left hand corner and search for the book. I add it to my cart and purchase it right then and there. Before I walk out to my car, I have the book on my phone ready for my hour commute next week. I was extremely proud of myself for having taken steps to implement the message...

I don't know what I <em>thought</em> the book was going to do for me. I don't think I actually thought that out at all. I started listening to the book on Tuesday but didn't get very far, and then again this morning. I'm 30 mins into a 7 hour book and I found I was ready to quit listening out of fustration. Let me explain.

I grew up in a very religious home (and I say that with no negative tone or judgmental thoughts, simply as a statement of fact). I also went to a two year pentecostal Bible college in Ohio. I've been around a lot of people who "witness" their faith in a variety of ways that "turn me off".

I call it volley, set, spike evangelism. Lets say you and I are in a conversation about how we deal with weeds that goes like this:

<strong>You:</strong> I got a letter from my HOA about the weeds in my yard.

<strong>Me:</strong> Really? I just spent most of Saturday dealing with the weeds in mine. [volley] Do you have a weed service? [Set]

<strong>You:</strong> No, I've been thinking about it.

<strong>Me:</strong> Did you know that Jesus can pull the weeds of sin out of your life if you let him? <strong>[SPIKE!]</strong>

The book has several stories highlighted as examples for how we can share our faith, and when the author described a Volley-Set-Spike style example as an acceptable model - I was ready to throw it out in its entirety. Before I dismissed the issue, I called Jason Fisher, a close personal friend.

I told him my thoughts on volley-set-spike methods and my thoughts on how I'm <em>already</em> fulfilling the Great Commission as I talk to my co-workers about my faith in casual conversations. That is when it hit me. I mean like a big smack in the face, anvil over the head hit me... Just walk across the room.

Thirty minutes into the book and I had only now realized that I'm falling short in my execution of the Great Commission by failing to follow those five words on the cover of this book. Jason was telling me how he makes a point to be in circles of people that are outside of his church circles. Places where he can have an opportunity to share his faith to someone that doesn't already share his faith. I don't do that.

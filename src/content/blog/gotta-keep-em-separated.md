I went to Bible College. I have a lot of ministers on my facebook stream as well as a lot of church attenders. I have a lot of religious friends. This group tend to have conservative views on both fiscal and social issues.

I also have atheist / agnostic friends from high school. These guys don't usually go to church and typically have liberal views on both fiscal and social issues.

I don't fit perfect in either bucket. Don't get me wrong; I'm not suggesting I'm special by any means and I'm not a moderate. I fit into a libertarian bucket pretty well but not perfectly. I'm fiscally conservative in almost every case but do believe in some government  funded social programs like education.

I am against doctor performed abortion in every case (yes every case) but I am against government defined marriage (gay marriage doesn't personally bother me). I believe God created the earth, but have no problem believing its possible that He did that billions and billions of years ago and dinosaurs once roamed the earth, and people eventually evolved from something else.

What's my point in blogging this?

I recently started performing at open mics as a standup comic. Its something I've always wanted to do, and now that I've started doing it, I don't know where I want to take it. I LOVE doing stand-up. The more I preform, the more I want to preform. The issue is the people I meet at a comedy club don't appear compatible with the church people already in my life. But I'm not looking to dump old friends or relationships over standup... I don't think.

I don't compartmentalize very well. I don't keep segmented portions of my life from each other. My work life and my personal life are often the same thing, my private life and my public life usually overlap. I'm an open book and I don't know why. I always have been, despite how often I wish I weren't.

My brother-in-law recently said, "Dale, if you ever wanted to cheat on my sister, she would have to be friends with your new girlfriend or you couldn't pull it off." I think he is right.

My inability to compartmentalize is a major issue if I want to maintain relationships with both circles. Its not the comics wouldn't be able to get along with church people - its that hanging with comics brings out a side of me that I haven't shown my church friends; a side they wouldn't approve of.

Its easy to say, if they don't approve of who you are, dump 'em. But I'm not ready to lose friends of 10, 15, and even 20 years over this new hobby. Not to mention that this is all internal dialog at this point; my church friends may not have any issue with the side of me that I've not displayed before.

I recently posted a meme regarding the ACA requiring catholics to purchase insurance for their employees that included birth control. When I shared it, I was thinking about the catholic church but later learned (from many angry comments on my post) that Hobby Lobby was in the news having challenged the ACA in court because they did not want to offer birth control options to their employees because the owners were Catholic.

The problem is that while I feel companies should be able to freely choose what benefits they do or don't offer, something about Hobby Lobby's argument just felt off.

I agree with their end goal but not with their method to get there. They are principally arguing that the terms of employment (in this particular case, health benefits) should be subject to the arbitrary belief system of the employer. When I tried to articulate this with a couple of my friends I failed.

Employment in most of America is at will - meaning either party can end the relationship for any reason - however there is an implied contract that the employee is hired to do a particular job at a particular pay. If the employer fires an employee without cause then even in a state that is considered an at will employment state, there is likely to be a lawsuit.

My concern is if the religious conviction of a non-religion based enterprise is a valid reason to not offer a particular benefit, companies could change existing benefits under the same pretense. A change in ownership, leadership, or even a stated change in current leaders' convictions could be then considered valid reasons for a company to drop existing coverage options for employees. This feels like a breach of contract (albeit an implied contract). 

I recognize the slippery slope element to my concern however I feel the principle set by this case is a bad one.

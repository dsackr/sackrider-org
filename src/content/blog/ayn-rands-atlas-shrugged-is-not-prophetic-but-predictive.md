Ayn Rand is both praised and persecuted for her novel Atlas Shrugged. I'm not going to go into any deep review of the book but I wanted to jot down two ideas I have from the book so far (I'm over half way through).

First is a general statement - I don't believe the world around us resembles the world Ayn Rand describes. There are amazing similarities but large differences as well. I believe she was predicting a world left to the "looters" but this world has not been left to the looters. Instead the producers have found a way to take advantage of the looters.

Atlas hasn't shrugged. In fact, I would say the world doesn't really have a group of producers that resemble the broad shoulders of Atlas. In Ayn's world, the rich are the producers. In this world, wealth is no indication of virtue. Ayn never suggests (at least not through the first half of the book) that the poor are looters necessarily.
